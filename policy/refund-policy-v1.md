# Consumer Returns and Refunds Policy — v1

The corpus the agent reasons against. Every `###` section is one retrievable clause and its
number is its id, so a decision can cite `6.1` and a reviewer can open this file and read the
same words the model saw. Chunking happens on those headings — see
`scripts/embed_policy_corpus.py`.

Deliberately written as a real policy rather than as a list of the sentences the agent needs:
most of it is ordinary retail prose that has nothing to do with any planted case. A corpus
containing only the decision-critical clauses would make keyword search look better than it
is, and L2.6's whole question is whether searching by meaning beats searching by word.

Amounts are in IDR. No customer, seller or staff member named here is real.

## 1. Scope and definitions

### 1.1 What this policy covers
This policy governs requests to return goods bought from the Store and requests for money
back on those goods. It applies to orders placed through the website, the mobile
application, and the telephone order desk. It does not govern warranty service after the
return period, which is handled under the manufacturer's own terms.

### 1.2 Who may request a refund
A request may be made by the account holder who placed the order, or by the recipient of a
gift where the sender has supplied a gift receipt. Requests from anyone else are declined
without prejudice, and the account holder is invited to make the request themselves.

### 1.3 Terms used in this document
A *return* is the physical movement of goods back to the Store. A *refund* is the movement of
money back to the customer. An *exchange* replaces one item with another of equal value and
creates no refund. *Store credit* is a balance held against the account and is not money.

## 2. Return windows

### 2.1 Standard window
A return may be requested within 30 days of the date the goods were delivered. The delivery
date is taken from the carrier's record, not from the date of the order, and not from the
date the customer says they opened the parcel.

### 2.2 Requests made after the window has closed
A request made more than 30 days after delivery is not automatically refused, but it cannot
be approved automatically either. It is passed to a reviewer, who may allow it where the
delay is explained by illness, travel, or a delay in the customer discovering the problem.

### 2.3 Extended window for seasonal purchases
Goods bought between 1 November and 24 December may be returned until 31 January of the
following year. The extension exists so that gifts bought early are not disadvantaged, and
it does not extend any other deadline in this policy.

## 3. Eligibility by reason

### 3.1 Change of mind
Where a customer simply no longer wants the goods, a refund is given less the cost of return
carriage. The goods must be unused and must come back in the packaging they arrived in. This
is the only reason under which the Store deducts carriage from the sum returned.

### 3.2 The wrong item was sent
Where the goods delivered are not the goods ordered, the customer pays nothing towards
carriage in either direction and the full sum is returned. The Store will normally offer to
send the correct item before it offers money back.

### 3.3 Goods that did not work on arrival
Where goods fail on first use, or fail to start at all, the full sum is returned including any
carriage the customer paid originally. Where several unrelated customers report the same
failure on the same product line within a short period, that pattern is treated as
supporting the account the customer has given.

### 3.4 Goods harmed in transit
Where goods arrive visibly harmed and the outer packaging shows matching harm, the claim is
settled in the customer's favour and the Store pursues the carrier itself. The customer is
asked, but not required, to photograph the packaging before disposing of it.

## 4. Condition and packaging

### 4.1 Unused and complete
Goods must come back complete, with every accessory, manual and free gift that was supplied
with them. A return that is missing a component is not refused outright; the value of the
missing component is deducted and the customer is told what was deducted and why.

### 4.2 Original packaging
Packaging must be intact enough for the goods to be sold again. Opening a box carefully does
not disqualify a return. Destroying a box, removing a security seal, or discarding a fitted
insert does, except where the reason for return is that the goods themselves were faulty.

### 4.3 Hygiene and personal care
Cosmetics, earrings, underwear, swimwear and any product applied to skin or hair cannot come
back once the seal is broken, whatever the reason given, unless the goods were faulty when
supplied. This restriction is required of the Store by public health rules and cannot be
waived by a reviewer.

### 4.4 Assembled and modified goods
Furniture that has been assembled, and any product that has been engraved, cut, altered or
professionally installed, cannot be returned under change of mind. Faults in such goods are
handled under section 3.3.

## 5. Goods that cannot be returned

### 5.1 Perishable, bespoke and downloadable goods
Food, flowers, plants, made-to-order goods, and anything delivered as a download or a licence
key are outside this policy entirely. Faults in these are handled case by case and always by
a person.

### 5.2 Gift cards and vouchers
Gift cards, e-vouchers and promotional credit cannot be returned and cannot be converted into
money, including where they were bought by mistake and never used. A gift card bought with a
stolen instrument is cancelled without refund to anyone.

### 5.3 Clearance and final-sale goods
Goods marked *final sale* at the moment of purchase carry no change-of-mind right. They carry
every other right in this policy, including the right to a refund where they were faulty or
never arrived, and a reviewer may not treat the marking as a reason to refuse such a claim.

## 6. Problems with delivery

### 6.1 Goods that never arrived
Where the carrier's record shows no confirmation of delivery and no signature was captured,
the sum is returned in full. Where the record does show confirmation but the customer says
nothing reached them, the case goes to a person, who will weigh the carrier's evidence
against the customer's history with the Store.

### 6.2 Goods left somewhere the customer did not choose
Where a carrier has left goods with a neighbour, in a locker, or in an unsecured place
without the customer's instruction, the Store treats the goods as undelivered and does not
ask the customer to go looking for them.

### 6.3 A parcel that arrived with nothing in it
Where a customer reports that a parcel was empty or its main item was absent, the claim is
always reviewed by a person and is never settled automatically. The weight recorded when the
parcel left the warehouse is compared with the weight of what was handed back, and both are
put in front of the reviewer: a returned parcel far lighter than the despatched one means the
goods have not come back, and a returned weight close to the despatched weight contradicts
the account given. Neither result refuses the claim outright.

### 6.4 Orders that arrived in several parts
Large orders are frequently despatched in more than one parcel, and the tracking for each
parcel is separate. A customer reporting missing goods is told what remains in transit before
any claim is opened, because most such reports are resolved by the second parcel arriving.

## 7. How and when money is returned

### 7.1 Method and timing
Money goes back to the instrument it came from, and reaches the customer within 14 days of
the request being approved. The Store does not return money to a different card, a different
account, or a third party, whatever the reason offered.

### 7.2 Store credit as an alternative
A customer may ask for store credit instead, in which case the balance is available
immediately rather than in 14 days. Credit taken in place of money cannot later be converted
back into money.

### 7.3 Where the original payment never completed
Where the payment for an order was never captured, or has already been reversed by the
customer's bank, no money is returned automatically. These cases go to a person every time,
because returning money against a payment that was never taken creates a loss that no
subsequent step recovers.

### 7.4 Currency, rounding and fees levied by others
Sums are returned in the currency charged. Where a bank or intermediary has levied its own
charge on the original payment, that charge is not part of the sum returned, and the Store
cannot recover it on the customer's behalf.

## 8. Partial refunds and deductions

### 8.1 Handling charge on returned goods
A handling charge of 5% of the item price, capped at 50,000, may be applied to change-of-mind
returns of large or awkward goods. It is not applied where the goods were faulty, were not
what was ordered, or never arrived.

### 8.2 Who pays for carriage
The customer pays carriage on change-of-mind returns. The Store pays in every other case. A
prepaid label is offered wherever the Store is paying, and a customer who has already paid
carriage in a case where the Store should have is reimbursed on request.

### 8.3 Price reductions after purchase
Where the price of goods falls within 7 days of purchase, the difference is refunded on
request without the goods coming back. This does not apply to prices reduced as part of a
time-limited sale event.

## 9. Misuse of this policy

### 9.1 Customers returning far more than their peers
Where a customer's rate of return sits in the highest tenth of comparable accounts on the
same tier, their claims are reviewed by a person regardless of the sums involved. The
comparison is made within the tier, because rates differ legitimately between tiers.

### 9.2 The same claim made repeatedly
Where an account has made the same kind of claim three or more times in twelve months, the
fourth is reviewed by a person. Repetition is not evidence of dishonesty and is not treated
as such; it is simply the point at which automatic handling stops being appropriate.

### 9.3 Large sums
Any request above 5,000,000 requires a person's approval, whatever the reason given and
however strong the supporting evidence. No automated step may approve a sum above this line.

### 9.4 Goods used for a single occasion and then sent back
Where goods show signs of having been used once — a garment carrying the marks of an evening
out, a tool bearing the traces of one job — the change-of-mind right does not apply, because
the goods can no longer be sold as new. Such cases are decided by a person, who may allow the
return where the signs are ambiguous.

### 9.5 Claims that contradict themselves
Where the account a customer gives cannot be reconciled with the Store's own records, the
case is passed to a person with both versions attached. It is not refused on the strength of
the contradiction alone, because records are wrong often enough to matter.

## 10. Security of the account

### 10.1 Recent changes to where goods go and how they are paid for
Where the delivery address and the payment instrument on an account were both changed within
30 days of the order in question, the case is treated as a possible compromise of the account
and is held for a person to look at. The account holder is contacted through a channel that
predates the changes.

### 10.2 Orders the account holder does not recognise
A customer who reports an order they did not place is not asked to return anything. The order
is cancelled, the sum reversed, and the matter handed to the team that deals with compromised
accounts rather than to returns.

## 11. Review by a person, and escalation

### 11.1 When a case stops being automatic
A case is passed to a person where evidence is missing or contradictory, where the sum exceeds
the line in 9.3, where the account carries a security hold under section 10, or where any part
of the assessment could not be completed. The list is not exhaustive: a reviewer may take any
case on their own initiative.

### 11.2 How long review takes
A case passed to a person is answered within 48 hours. Where the Store misses that deadline,
the customer is told it has been missed and what is being done, rather than left waiting.

### 11.3 Decisions reached without a person
Some requests are approved without a person looking at them. No request is *refused* without a
person looking at it. A customer may ask what evidence was considered, and may ask for any
automatic approval to be reviewed by a person instead.

## 12. Goods sold by other sellers

### 12.1 Orders fulfilled by a seller rather than the Store
Where goods were despatched by a third-party seller through the Store's platform, that
seller's own returns terms apply to condition and to carriage. The windows in section 2 and
the protections in sections 6 and 10 apply regardless of who despatched the goods.

### 12.2 When the Store steps in
Where a seller has not answered a return request within 5 working days, the Store settles the
claim itself and recovers the sum from the seller. The customer is not asked to wait for the
seller a second time.

## 13. Regional variation and statutory rights

### 13.1 Rights that exist without this policy
Nothing here removes a right the customer has under consumer law, and where local law is more
generous than this document, local law applies. A reviewer who is unsure which is more
generous applies the more generous.

### 13.2 Returns that cross a border
Goods despatched from outside the customer's own territory may take longer to return and may
attract duties, which the Store bears where the return is not a change of mind. The windows
in section 2 run from delivery, not from despatch, in every territory.
